    <div class="research-copy">
      <div class="research-kicker">01 · Understanding plasma</div>
      <h2>Fundamental plasma physics</h2>
      <p>We investigate how magnetized plasmas generate and reorganize fields, convert energy, and approach—or remain far from—equilibrium. Our <strong>canonical-vorticity framework</strong> treats magnetic field and fluid vorticity as parts of a single dynamical quantity, providing a unified view of magnetic reconnection, magnetogenesis, and related vorticity dynamics.</p>
      <p>At kinetic scales, we connect <strong>single-particle trajectories</strong> to evolving <strong>phase-space distributions</strong> and then to macroscopic moments such as density, flow, current, and temperature. This micro-to-macro approach underpins our work on non-equilibrium relaxation, plasma thermodynamics, and coherent wave–particle interactions.</p>
      <ul class="research-focus-list" aria-label="Fundamental plasma physics topics">
        <li>Magnetic reconnection</li>
        <li>Magnetogenesis</li>
        <li>Canonical-vorticity dynamics (QHD)</li>
        <li>Non-equilibrium dynamics</li>
        <li>Plasma thermodynamics</li>
        <li>Wave–particle interactions</li>
      </ul>

  <section class="research-topic research-topic--reverse" id="applied-plasma-physics">
    <div class="research-copy">
      <div class="research-kicker">02 · Putting plasma to work</div>
      <h2>Applied plasma physics</h2>
      <p>We translate fundamental plasma processes into strategies for fusion energy, spacecraft propulsion, and space-environment forecasting. In magnetic-confinement fusion, we study wave activity, resonant scattering, and the <strong>firewall effect</strong> as routes to controlling energetic and runaway electrons. We also investigate reconnection and beam-driven turbulence in compact, non-inductively formed plasma configurations relevant to novel fusion concepts.</p>
      <p>For space applications, we use reconnection and coherent wave–particle dynamics to improve the physical basis for space-weather prediction and control. We also explore electric-propulsion concepts in which asymmetric magnetic reconnection converts normally bidirectional Alfvénic exhaust into <strong>net thrust</strong>.</p>
      <ul class="research-focus-list" aria-label="Applied plasma physics topics">
        <li>Nuclear fusion</li>
        <li>Runaway suppression</li>
        <li>Novel fusion reactor concepts</li>
        <li>Space propulsion</li>
        <li>Space-weather prediction and control</li>
      </ul>

  <section class="research-topic" id="interdisciplinary">
    <div class="research-copy">
      <div class="research-kicker">03 · Across disciplines</div>
      <h2>Interdisciplinary</h2>
      <p>We combine plasma kinetic theory with ideas from gravitation and data science. The <strong>Einstein–Vlasov framework</strong> links collisionless phase-space dynamics to evolving spacetime geometry, while gravitoelectromagnetism provides a carefully bounded analogy between electromagnetic fields and gravitational inertial or tidal effects.</p>
      <p>Our scientific-machine-learning efforts use <strong>physics-informed neural networks (PINNs)</strong> to reconstruct canonical-vorticity fields and non-ideal terms from sparse spacecraft observations. A complementary direction develops data-driven closures that carry kinetic information into reduced plasma-fluid simulations without the full cost of a particle model.</p>
      <ul class="research-focus-list" aria-label="Interdisciplinary research topics">
        <li>Gravitoelectromagnetism and Einstein–Vlasov theory</li>
        <li>PINN-integrated data reconstruction</li>
        <li>Plasma simulation with data-driven closures</li>
      </ul>
